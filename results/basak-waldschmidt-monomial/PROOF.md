# Negative Answer to Basak–Bijender–Kumar Question 4.14 (`arXiv:2611.19424v1`)

## 1. Statement

Let $R = K[z_1, \dots, z_N]$ be a polynomial ring over a field $K$. For a nonzero homogeneous ideal $J \subset R$, let $\alpha(J) = \min\{d \ge 0 : J_d \ne 0\}$ denote its initial degree. For a monomial ideal $I \subset R$, let $e = \max\{\mathrm{ht}(P) : P \in \mathrm{Ass}(I)\}$ be its big-height, $I^{(m)} = \bigcap_{P \in \mathrm{Ass}(I)} (I^m R_P \cap R)$ its $m$-th symbolic power, and $\hat{\alpha}(I) = \lim_{m \to \infty} \alpha(I^{(m)}) / m = \inf_{m \ge 1} \alpha(I^{(m)}) / m$ its Waldschmidt constant.

In [arXiv:2611.19424v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19424v1) (*On the Waldschmidt constant of monomial ideals*, 14 Nov 2026), Basak, Bijender, and Kumar ask:

> **Question 4.14.** *Let $I \subset R$ be a monomial ideal of big-height $e$. Is the lower bound*
> $$\hat{\alpha}(I) \ge \frac{\alpha(I) + e - 1}{e + 1}$$
> *always satisfied?*

We prove that the answer is **no**, constructing an explicit infinite family of unmixed monomial ideals violating this bound.

**Theorem 1.** *Let $T \ge 4$ and $r \ge 1$ be integers satisfying $r(T - 3) > T(T - 1)$ (in particular, $r \ge T$). Let $B = \{b \in \mathbb{N}^r : \sum_{j=1}^r b_j = T - 1\}$, and in the polynomial ring $R = K[x_1, \dots, x_r, y_b : b \in B]$ define*
$$q_b = \bigl(x_1^{b_1 + 1}, \dots, x_r^{b_r + 1},\, y_b\bigr), \qquad I = \bigcap_{b \in B} q_b.$$
*Then:*
1. *The decomposition $I = \bigcap_{b \in B} q_b$ is irredundant, every $q_b^m$ is $P_b$-primary for $P_b = (x_1, \dots, x_r, y_b)$, $\mathrm{Ass}(I) = \mathrm{Min}(I) = \{P_b : b \in B\}$, every associated prime has height $e = r + 1$, and $I^{(m)} = \bigcap_{b \in B} q_b^m$ for all $m \ge 1$ under every standard definition of symbolic powers.*
2. *$\alpha(I) = T$.*
3. *For every $m \ge 1$, $\alpha(I^{(m)}) \ge \frac{2r}{2r - T + 1} m$, while for every $k \ge 1$, $\alpha(I^{((2r - T + 1)k)}) \le 2rk$. Hence*
   $$\hat{\alpha}(I) = \frac{2r}{2r - T + 1} < \frac{T + r}{r + 2} = \frac{\alpha(I) + e - 1}{e + 1}.$$

For $(T, r) = (4, 13)$ ($|B| = \binom{15}{3} = 455$ components, $468$ variables), we obtain $\alpha(I) = 4$, $e = 14$, and
$$\hat{\alpha}(I) = \frac{26}{23} = \frac{390}{345} < \frac{391}{345} = \frac{17}{15} = \frac{\alpha(I) + e - 1}{e + 1}.$$

## 2. Proof

Fix $T \ge 4$ and $r \ge 1$ with $r(T - 3) > T(T - 1)$. Note that $r(T - 3) > T(T - 1) > (T - 1)(T - 3)$ forces $r \ge T$ (in fact $r \ge 11$ for all valid $(T, r)$). Write $w_j^b = b_j + 1 \ge 1$ and $P_b = (x_1, \dots, x_r, y_b)$.

### Lemma 2.1 (Monomial membership in $q_b^m$)
*A polynomial $f \in R$ lies in $q_b^m$ if and only if every monomial $x^c y^d$ appearing in $f$ with nonzero coefficient satisfies*
$$\sum_{j=1}^r \left\lfloor \frac{c_j}{b_j + 1} \right\rfloor + d_b \ge m.$$

*Proof.* Let $J_m \subset R$ be the $K$-subspace spanned by all monomials $x^c y^d$ satisfying $\sum_{j=1}^r \lfloor c_j / (b_j + 1) \rfloor + d_b \ge m$. Since $\lfloor (c_j + c'_j)/w \rfloor \ge \lfloor c_j/w \rfloor + \lfloor c'_j/w \rfloor$, we have $J_{m_1} \cdot J_{m_2} \subseteq J_{m_1 + m_2}$ and each $J_m$ is an ideal. Every generator $x_j^{b_j+1}$ or $y_b$ of $q_b$ lies in $J_1$, so $q_b \subseteq J_1$ and by induction $q_b^m \subseteq J_m$. Conversely, if $x^c y^d \in J_m$, choose integers $0 \le e_j \le \lfloor c_j / (b_j + 1) \rfloor$ and $0 \le e_0 \le d_b$ with $e_0 + \sum_{j=1}^r e_j = m$; then $\bigl(\prod_{j=1}^r (x_j^{b_j+1})^{e_j}\bigr) y_b^{e_0} \in q_b^m$ divides $x^c y^d$, so $x^c y^d \in q_b^m$. Thus $q_b^m = J_m$. $\square$

### Lemma 2.2 (Primary decomposition, big-height, and symbolic powers)
*For each $b \in B$ and $m \ge 1$, the ideal $q_b^m$ is $P_b$-primary, and $P_b$ is a prime ideal of height $r + 1$. Moreover, $\mathrm{Ass}(I) = \mathrm{Min}(I) = \{P_b : b \in B\}$, the big-height of $I$ is $e = r + 1$, and $I^{(m)} = \bigcap_{b \in B} q_b^m$ for every $m \ge 1$.*

*Proof.* The quotient $R / P_b \cong K[y_{b'} : b' \ne b]$ is an integral domain, so $P_b$ is prime of height $r + 1$ (generated by $r + 1$ distinct indeterminates).

To see that $q_b^m$ is $P_b$-primary: clearly $q_b^m \subseteq P_b$ and every generator of $P_b$ has a power in $q_b^m$ (so $\sqrt{q_b^m} = P_b$). Suppose $f, g \in R$ satisfy $fg \in q_b^m$ and $g \notin P_b$. Write $f = f_1 + f_2$ where $f_1 \in q_b^m$ and every monomial in $f_2$ lies outside $q_b^m$, and similarly $g = g_1 + g_2$ where $g_1 \in P_b$ and $g_2 \in K[y_{b'} : b' \ne b] \setminus \{0\}$. Suppose for contradiction that $f_2 \ne 0$, and let $f_2^{(\delta)} \ne 0$ be its homogeneous component of minimal total $(x, y_b)$-degree $\delta$. Every monomial in $f_2 g_1$ has $(x, y_b)$-degree at least $\delta + 1$, whereas every monomial in $f_2^{(\delta)} g_2$ has $(x, y_b)$-degree $\delta$. Viewing $R = K[y_{b'} : b' \ne b][x_1, \dots, x_r, y_b]$, since $K[y_{b'} : b' \ne b]$ is an integral domain and $g_2 \ne 0$, the product $f_2^{(\delta)} g_2$ is nonzero and every monomial appearing in it shares its $(x, y_b)$-exponent with a monomial of $f_2^{(\delta)}$. By Lemma 2.1, no such monomial lies in $q_b^m$, contradicting $f_2 g = fg - f_1 g \in q_b^m$. Therefore $f_2 = 0$, i.e., $f \in q_b^m$.

The primes $P_b$ all have $r + 1$ variables and are distinct, hence pairwise incomparable. The intersection $I = \bigcap_{b \in B} q_b$ is irredundant because $U_b := \prod_{b' \ne b} y_{b'}$ belongs to every $q_{b'}$ ($b' \ne b$) while $U_b \notin q_b$ by Lemma 2.1. Thus $\mathrm{Ass}(I) = \mathrm{Min}(I) = \{P_b : b \in B\}$ and $e = r + 1$.

Finally, for any $b \in B$ and $m \ge 1$: if $f \in I^m R_{P_b} \cap R$, then $u f \in I^m \subseteq q_b^m$ for some $u \in R \setminus P_b$; since $q_b^m$ is $P_b$-primary, $f \in q_b^m$. Conversely, if $f \in q_b^m$, then $U_b \notin P_b$ and $U_b \cdot q_b \subseteq I$, so $U_b^m f \in I^m$, whence $f \in I^m R_{P_b} \cap R$. Therefore $I^m R_{P_b} \cap R = q_b^m$, and $I^{(m)} = \bigcap_{b \in B} q_b^m$. $\square$

### Lemma 2.3 (Initial degree $\alpha(I) = T$)
*We have $\alpha(I) = T$.*

*Proof.* Since every $b \in B$ satisfies $b_1 \le T - 1$, we have $b_1 + 1 \le T$, so $x_1^T \in q_b$ for all $b \in B$, giving $x_1^T \in I$ and $\alpha(I) \le T$.

Conversely, let $x^c y^d \in I$ be any monomial. If $|c| \ge T$, then $\deg(x^c y^d) = |c| + |d| \ge T$. Suppose $s := |c| \le T - 1$, and set $B_c = \{b \in B : b_j \ge c_j\ \forall j\}$. For every $b \in B_c$, $c_j < b_j + 1$ for all $j$, so Lemma 2.1 ($m = 1$) forces $d_b \ge 1$. Hence $|d| \ge |B_c|$. Writing $b' = b - c \in \mathbb{N}^r$, $B_c$ is in bijection with compositions of $T - 1 - s$ into $r$ nonnegative parts:
- If $s = T - 1$, then $b' = 0$ is unique, so $|B_c| = 1$ and $s + |B_c| = T$.
- If $s \le T - 2$, then for every $j \in \{1, \dots, r\}$, adding $(T - 1 - s)$ to coordinate $j$ gives $r$ distinct vectors in $B_c$, so $|B_c| \ge r \ge T \ge T - s$ and $s + |B_c| \ge T$.
Thus every monomial in $I$ has degree at least $T$, proving $\alpha(I) = T$. $\square$

### Lemma 2.4 (Exact Waldschmidt constant $\hat{\alpha}(I) = \frac{2r}{2r - T + 1}$)
1. *For every $k \ge 1$, the monomial $u_k = \prod_{j=1}^r x_j^{2k}$ (of degree $2rk$) lies in $I^{((2r - T + 1)k)}$.*
2. *For every $m \ge 1$ and every monomial $x^c y^d \in I^{(m)}$, we have $(2r - T + 1)(|c| + |d|) \ge 2r m$.*
3. *Consequently, $\hat{\alpha}(I) = \frac{2r}{2r - T + 1}$.*

*Proof.*
1. Fix $b \in B$ and $k \ge 1$. Let $p = |\{j : b_j = 0\}|$, $q = |\{j : b_j = 1\}|$, and $\beta = |\{j : b_j \ge 2\}|$, so $p + q + \beta = r$ and $q + 2\beta \le \sum_{j=1}^r b_j = T - 1$. Then
   $$\sum_{j=1}^r \left\lfloor \frac{2k}{b_j + 1} \right\rfloor \ge 2k p + k q = k(2p + q) = k(2r - q - 2\beta) \ge (2r - T + 1)k.$$
   By Lemma 2.1, $u_k \in q_b^{((2r - T + 1)k)}$ for all $b \in B$, so $u_k \in I^{((2r - T + 1)k)}$.
2. Let $O = \{b \in B : b_j \in \{0, 1\}\ \forall j\}$ be the set of $\binom{r}{T - 1}$ binary vectors of weight $T - 1$ (valid since $r \ge T$). For each $b \in O$, Lemma 2.1 yields
   $$\sum_{j=1}^r \frac{c_j}{b_j + 1} + d_b \ge \sum_{j=1}^r \left\lfloor \frac{c_j}{b_j + 1} \right\rfloor + d_b \ge m.$$
   Summing over all $b \in O$: for each coordinate $j \in \{1, \dots, r\}$, exactly $\binom{r - 1}{T - 2}$ vectors in $O$ have $b_j = 1$ (weight $1/2$) and $\binom{r - 1}{T - 1}$ have $b_j = 0$ (weight $1$), so
   $$\lambda := \sum_{b \in O} \frac{1}{b_j + 1} = \frac{1}{2}\binom{r - 1}{T - 2} + \binom{r - 1}{T - 1} = \frac{2r - T + 1}{2r} \binom{r}{T - 1} = \frac{2r - T + 1}{2r} |O|.$$
   Note that $\lambda \ge 1$ since $\binom{r - 1}{T - 1} \ge 1$. Therefore
   $$\lambda |c| + \sum_{b \in O} d_b \ge |O| m \implies \lambda(|c| + |d|) \ge \lambda |c| + \sum_{b \in O} d_b \ge |O| m,$$
   which simplifies to $(2r - T + 1)(|c| + |d|) \ge 2r m$.
3. By (1) and (2), $\frac{2r}{2r - T + 1} \le \frac{\alpha(I^{(m)})}{m}$ for every $m \ge 1$, while $\frac{\alpha(I^{((2r - T + 1)k)})}{(2r - T + 1)k} \le \frac{2r}{2r - T + 1}$ for every $k \ge 1$; since $I^{(m+1)} \subseteq I^{(m)}$, for arbitrary $m \ge 1$ taking $k = \lceil m / (2r - T + 1) \rceil$ gives $\lim_{m \to \infty} \alpha(I^{(m)}) / m = \inf_{m \ge 1} \alpha(I^{(m)}) / m = \frac{2r}{2r - T + 1}$. $\square$

### Completion of Proof of Theorem 1
Since $2r - T + 1 > 0$ and $r + 2 > 0$,
$$\frac{2r}{2r - T + 1} < \frac{T + r}{r + 2} \iff 2r(r + 2) < (T + r)(2r - T + 1) \iff r(T - 3) > T(T - 1),$$
which holds by hypothesis. $\square$
